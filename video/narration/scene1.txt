Sapporo, Japan — my city. In winter, snow is not just a chore. For many people here, it's a daily risk. Five hundred sixty-six thousand, seven hundred ten residents are sixty-five or older — twenty-nine point four percent. Nearly one hundred twenty-two thousand seniors live alone. The city's welfare snow-removal service has strict eligibility — and too few helpers to go around. Every winter, about one thousand nine hundred people are hospitalized after falls on snowy streets. Nearly half are seventy or older. Last winter in Hokkaido, ten people died clearing snow — every one of them while shoveling. On our street: an elderly neighbor, alone in the snow. When we asked why — she said: I have no one to ask.
